### Pain

Legal’s contract work is spread across entity/department Excel trackers, Google Docs, email, and printed routing slips. From the discovery conversation, the team repeatedly faces:

- **Tracking** — Where is the contract physically? Who has custody? What stage is it in?
- **Expiry** — No reliable centralized monitoring; renewal exposure is easy to miss.
- **Storage and retrieval** — Hard to find the latest version versus drafts; historical lookup requires manual chase.
- **Status visibility** — Requesters and managers lack a single view; answers come from email round-trips.
- **Notifications** — Status updates depend on someone remembering to email; no consistent alert rhythm for expiry.

**Pain in one line:** Nobody can reliably answer *where a contract is, who owns it, which copy is current, or when it expires* without manual reconciliation.

### Promise

DSSI delivers an **Operational Gap Audit** and a **fixed-scope pilot**: one **system of record** for contract **status**, **physical custody**, **latest copy pointer**, and **expiry**—with **light, assistive AI**—while **keeping Google Docs for drafting** and **printed LEG-FORM-5 routing** for internal review.

This is **not** a 12-month CLM rip-and-replace. It is a thin operations layer Legal controls, sized to prove value before any Execom-funded rollout.

### Differentiation

| Alternative | Limitation | DSSI pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Excel + Google Docs + email** | Multiple truths per entity; no shared status timeline; expiry buried in cells; stakeholders must chase Legal. | One matter record, assignment pool, audit trail, expiry buckets (90/30/7), email on status change; Excel import for bootstrap—not permanent dual entry. |
| **Full enterprise CLM** | Long procurement, heavy change management, often pushes new drafting/redlining workflows; weak fit for wet-ink culture and existing GDoc practice. | Weeks-not-months pilot; preserves GDoc links and print routing PDF; optional digital routing **off** by default; scope capped to contract ops (not labor/criminal/civil trackers). |

## Objection FAQ

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| **“We need wet-ink and routing slips—not another digital tool.”** | The pilot’s primary path is **print-first**: LEG-FORM-5 PDF with signature blocks, **printed copy issued** recorded before Internal Review. Digital approvals stay **off** in production unless you enable them later. |
| **“AI is risky for Legal.”** | AI in the pilot is **assistive only**: status summaries from metadata, expiry flags, search suggestions, optional routing prefill—**no** legal advice, **no** auto-approval, **no** auto status changes. Disclaimers on every AI surface; attorneys remain in the loop. |
| **“We already have Excel; migration will be painful.”** | Pilot includes **thin import** (one entity export or bounded CSV) plus a short **parallel run**—champion commits pilot matters to the app, not a shadow sheet. Excel remains reference during bootstrap; goal is one system of record for pilot cohort. |
| **“Security and IT—where does data live?”** | Hosting, SSO, and data residency are **joint decisions** with GBPI IT (on-prem vs. cloud tenant). Pilot uses role-based access (requester / legal / manager), audit logging, and no public indexing of contract text unless counsel approves doc indexing policy. |
| **“This sounds like CLM—we’re not ready.”** | Scope is explicitly **not** full CLM: no playbook automation, no replacement of GDocs, no vendor portal, no case/labor/criminal trackers in pilot. It is **contract operations**: status, custody, pointers, expiry. |
| **“Requesters won’t adopt another login.”** | Requester value is immediate: submit once, **matter number**, status timeline, email on change—less back-and-forth than today. Training: one short session for requester champions in pilot departments. |
